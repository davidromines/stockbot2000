What this actually is

A pipeline that ingests US equity market data, computes indicators and fundamentals, generates and tests trading strategies, and runs the survivors forward as paper funds. A small amount of real money (~$89) trades alongside it in a Robinhood cash account.

Since 22 September the project has changed shape. The evolutionary search is frozen; a Strategy Factory now generates strategies from 40 economically motivated families, backtests them, stress-tests them and enrols the survivors in paper trading every morning. Backtests only admit a strategy. Forward evidence is the only thing that can qualify one. The trading engine that runs the five best qualified strategies in five ~$20 slots is built and runs every five minutes in market hours, in SIMULATION — all five slots in cash, because no strategy has yet earned one. Arming live trading is the owner's step, and a 26-item acceptance checklist says it is not ready (no SHADOW record yet).

The central finding still stands: every apparent edge this system has produced has, on investigation, been an artifact of how it was measured — or, now, a small forward sample in a market where the rule happened to work. Nothing has cleared a forward test.

The measurement bug ledger

Ordered by date, newest first. Every entry has the same shape: a result was believed, a measurement proved it false, and the belief was retracted. This is the part of the project an independent reviewer should read first.

2026-09-24 · risk_engine.py, execution.pynew

Every approved exit sold nothing

The risk engine returned early for sells without setting a size, and the execution engine builds orders from the risk engine's size, never the signal's. So every approved exit became an order with neither quantity nor notional: the simulator "filled" zero shares and a Robinhood order spec would have carried no size. Found while wiring the slot trader, which needs its stops to actually sell.

Every simulated exit, since 22 September
2026-09-24 · universe_layer_a.pynew

Dead companies were credited with their successors' prices

Tickers get reused. Matching a delisted company by ticker attached it to whoever holds the ticker today, and "has real prices" was true whenever any prices existed under it. All 426 priced dead companies with a delisting date had prices running about 2,000 days past it. The real dead-company sample fell from 676 to 158 once ownership required the prices to end near the company's own end.

The calibration sample, 77% impostors — caught before use
2026-09-24 · finsaber.pynew

An external dataset with two companies under one symbol

99 of FINSABER's 1,017 symbols alternate between two price levels day to day — CBE between $170 and $0.005, 761 times. Each bar is individually valid, so the import's row checks passed them, and the cross-dataset check only compared symbols both datasets share. A price-only momentum backtest gained 15 points a year from these names.

+15 points/yr of fiction — flagged, excluded
2026-09-24 · FINSABER filingsnew

Filing text dated before it could be traded on

FINSABER dates each 10-K and 10-Q on its SEC filed day. Against the SEC's own acceptance time and the next tradeable session, 89.7% are dated too early. Any use of that text must join to the tradeable date instead. The 5.4 million headlines have no reference timestamp at all and stay unusable as a signal.

Measured before anything used it
2026-09-24 · execution.pynew

Simulation could use up live trading's daily order limit

Order ids and daily throttles ignored the execution mode, so a SHADOW run of a SIMULATION decision was suppressed as a duplicate, and SIMULATION orders counted against the SHADOW — and later LIVE — daily order cap. Found by the end-to-end test.

Fixed before LIVE existed
2026-09-24 · paper_trading.py, leagues.pynew

Production worked only because of columns added by hand

Three paper_runs columns and one accounting table existed in the live database and in no code. A rebuilt database broke strategy enrolment. The end-to-end test, run on a fresh database for the first time, found both.

Fixed; now created in code
2026-09-24 · paper_trading.py → accounting.pynew

The paper engine flattered its own funds

Two defects. A held position with no bar that day was marked at its entry price, so a falling stock showed no loss until it traded again. And in 11 funds a repeated daily step overwrote positions already held, so cash and positions drifted apart. An independent accounting module now restates every fund on a liquidation basis, beside the original curves rather than over them.

Forward net across 23 funds: −$4.52 → −$49.59 (gross −$41.19, costs −$8.40).

~11× understatement of forward losses
2026-09-24 · value_metrics.pynew

Fundamentals were on two scales at once

A 10-Q reports a quarter's flow; a 10-K reports a year's. The metrics mixed them, so ROA and earnings yield jumped roughly fourfold after every annual report and fell back with the next quarterly. Every conviction screen ranked on that sawtooth. Flows are now annualised and both fundamental tables rebuilt.

Every fundamental ranking before 09-24
2026-09-23 · backfill.py, freshness.pynew

An intraday top-up stored in-progress bars as closes

Run during market hours, the top-up wrote 41 partial bars for the current session — permanent, because a later run sees the date as already present. Removed; the fetch now drops anything dated today in New York time, and the freshness gate fails on data newer than the market.

Caught the same day
2026-09-24 · dataset_compare.pynew

A validation check that would have invented a data problem

The first comparison against FINSABER's independent S&P 500 series flagged ~60% of overlapping bars as price discrepancies. Both series are dividend-adjusted, but anchored at different download dates, so their levels differ by a per-ticker factor while every return agrees. Compared by daily return: 99.5–99.8% agree within 0.5 pp in every year from 2001. The few real mismatches are reused tickers.

A false alarm, caught before anyone acted on it
2026-09-24 · stop_sweep.pynew

The project's one live lead did not survive its own test

Six paper funds share one entry rule and differ by stop width; tight stops led for two weeks. The pre-registered sweep over 2006–2019 found the rule loses net in all 30 stop × hold settings, and tight stops did no better than wide. The funds stay classified PROMISING / INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE, with the sweep attached to each record as a hashed artifact.

Lead refuted; record kept, not rewritten
2026-09-22 · seeds.py / lab_loop.sh

The search converged on a rule we had handed it

455 survivors, 85% containing "momentum + pullback", apparently corroborated by the best paper fund. Not independent: seeds.py injected a hand-written momentum_pullback rule every iteration, 231 survivors carry its literal constant 40, and the paper fund descends from the same seed.

Reported as the best finding, retracted in one hour
2026-09-15 · simulator.py, backtest.py, benchmark.py

Orders filled at the close that produced the signal

Entry rules read a bar's own close and the engines then filled at that same close — a price unobtainable until the session ends. Stops were perfect for the same reason. Controlled comparison: gross P&L +$1.63 → −$58.77. The strategy loses money before paying any spread.

Reversed the project's headline result
2026-09-15 · backfill.py, storage.stale_tickers

Data freshness measured against the table being refreshed

The top-up asked which tickers were behind MAX(date) in the table it existed to advance. Every downstream consumer worked a full session behind, every day, while the pipeline reported success.

Silent, daily, for an unknown duration
2026-09-15 · storage.py

One SQL line, two functions, one string replace

A string replace hit a SELECT line that appears verbatim in two functions. A runtime NameError killed a stage of the daily job.

2026-09-14 · daily_picks.py

Price and volume floors cannot see company size

The book bought TNON, a ~$3.5M company clearing the liquidity floor on $102M/day of blowoff volume after a 1-for-35 reverse split. It had no market cap on record, so "reject below the floor" waved it through. Unknown caps are now rejected.

2026-09-14 · conviction.py

Value ranks were size ranks in disguise

Book-to-market correlates −0.35 with log size, so ranking on it sorted toward small before cheap. Screens now rank within size buckets; two of three improved.

Fixed; measurably improved the screens

Questions for the reviewer

The areas where an outside opinion would change what gets built next.

Q1 · Methodology

Is the validation ladder sound, or elaborately self-deceiving?

A sealed holdout, a null surface, a random-genome control, and now authoritative accounting and cross-dataset checks. Every positive result so far has still turned out to be an artifact or noise. Is there a structural flaw, or is this what honest measurement of a weak signal looks like?

Q2 · Automation

Should a system with no demonstrated edge trade automatically?

The next build (Addendum A) runs the five best qualified strategies automatically, and leaves a slot in cash when nothing qualifies. Today nothing does. Are the qualification thresholds — 20 forward sessions, 10 closed trades, positive net, a drawdown cap — strict enough that "cash" stays the answer until something earns otherwise?

Q3 · Factory admission

Is the paper-trading gate too easy?

On its first daily-size run the factory admitted 8 of 12 strategies to paper trading after positive backtests, out-of-sample validation and robustness tests — all on survivorship-limited data. Admission costs only paper, but a crowded league dilutes the forward record the slots will be chosen from.

Q4 · Answered 09-24

Is stop width a real effect?

No. The sweep refuted it across all 30 settings over fourteen years.

— refuted
Q5 · Priority

Data or method?

The missing delisted companies remain the largest known bias. Against that, this week's bugs — accounting, fundamentals scale — were method errors again. Where does the next unit of effort go?
