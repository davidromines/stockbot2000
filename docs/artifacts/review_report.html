Stockbot2000

An autonomous system that discovers, tests and ranks trading strategies, and trades a small real Robinhood account with them: three slots hold medium-term strategies, two run same-day momentum Runners. This version is self-contained. Prediction: do the strategies pick winners?

No, on every forward measure available.

  • Live trades that ended by their own rule or stop: 7, mean −0.50%. Too few to mean anything alone.
  • The paper league is where prediction is actually tested:
    • The pre-registered forward_families_prospective is at 17 of 20 pooled entry dates (20,096 trades). All eight families are negative net, −0.91% to −6.22% per trade, and all are below SPY over the same days (excess −0.77% to −6.83%). Interim only; the verdict is due about early December.
    • The funnel (11-06): of 86 families with 20 or more forward trades, 4 have a positive honest mean, and none passes. The best-sampled positive one is momentum at +0.30% (37 entry dates, clustered t +0.3). lab_search is −1.03% per trade on 23,906 trades (clustered t −2.3).
  • The slot holders' own paper records are poor, and the ranking does not see them. Under evidence_mode: backtest_first (owner 10-14, kept 10-19) a strategy is scored on its backtest alone, so its score cannot move:

Execution: are trades carried out as intended?

  • Yes. The ledger matches Robinhood on all 25 days compared, and buys filled +0.02% against the decision-time quote on average (81 orders).
  • The first two medium-term stops fired at their computed levels.
  • No quote halts since the 10-21 fix; one Robinhood error stopped a single run on 11-05.
  • Defect: re-entry after a stop. OVV was sold on its stop at $61.45 on 11-05 and bought back by the same strategy 6 minutes later at $61.26 with a new, lower stop. The stop booked the loss but kept the exposure.
  • Defect: a false halt. The 5-minute reconciliation can catch a Runner order in flight. This happened once, on 10-28 (RWM), and the next run cleared it.

Turnover: is the system trading too much?

  • Not any more. Before 10-19, 17 of 24 medium-term exits were forced by slot reassignment, after a median of about 4 sessions against designed holds of 21–252.
  • Since 10-19: no forced exits. Both medium-term exits were stops after 16–17 sessions, and the first monthly review (11-02) released nothing.
  • The Runners make about 4 round trips a session at $5. Their cost is small, but so is any possible gain.

Allocation: is the money spread sensibly?

No. Single-name risk is the main open problem.

  • One stock per slot. Each slot is $20, about 26% of the account, so three single stocks carry the overnight risk.
  • Most of the loss is stock-specific. Regressing the 31 daily account returns on SPY gives a beta of 0.81 (correlation 0.56). The market explains about 3.3 points of the roughly 13.6-point summed daily loss; the remaining ~10.3 come from the stocks themselves. The daily excess return is −0.33% (t ≈ −1.5), so a negative edge is not established either.
  • The book beta cap (1.3) never bound. The book's beta is 0.75, but the cap measures market exposure only: NTLA has a beta of 2.1 and about 95% a year of stock-specific volatility, and OVV (oil, beta −0.35) fell 15% in one week.
  • Stops are wide on volatile names. NTLA's stop is 20% below its entry, OVV's 11% and MA's 7%; across the 10-19 holdings the range was 16–33%. One slot can lose $1–6 before its stop fires.
  • Basket slots are not live yet. Four names per slot with inverse-volatility weights would spread this risk, but they still run only in SIMULATION, where the first basket entries came on 11-05/06. There is no comparison yet.

  1. The machinery works. Fills, ledger, stops, reconciliation, alerts and the churn fix all behave as designed.
  2. There is no edge. The forward evidence is negative against both zero and SPY.
  3. The live loss since 10-19 is single-name risk in a falling market, on holders the ranking cannot judge by their forward record.

4Forward evidence

Forward evidence is the only measurement here without look-ahead and without the survivorship problem: strategies trade on data that did not exist when they were defined. There are three layers of it.

The paper league (diagnostic funnel, 11-06)

About 2,918 open paper funds trade every candidate strategy on live daily data (71,328 closed paper trades; the longest record is 40 sessions). The funnel reports each family four ways:

  • Gross: the price move as recorded.
  • Net: modelled costs deducted.
  • Honest: net, at next-open fills.
  • Cluster t: errors clustered by entry date and ticker, so a day or a stock counts once.

The shadow capital gate

Every day a 13-criterion gate (capital_gate.py, [9k1]) asks of every strategy with 20 or more honest trades whether it would deserve real capital. It checks data integrity, provenance, net return at realistic fills, independent evidence (20 own dates), uncertainty, a multiple-testing correction, stability, cost sensitivity, survivorship, correlation, downside, retroactive share and the prospective test. It enforces nothing. 0 of 1,497 are eligible, and 0 of the 3 slot holders. No strategy passes the multiple-testing criterion (C6).

5Architecture

There are three layers, and the code keeps them from calling each other. The research factory creates and backtests ideas. The league and ranking paper-trade and score them. Capital deployment trades only through the risk and execution layer. Research code cannot import the broker, and a regression test enforces it.

Design rules that bear load

  • Fail closed on unknowns. An unknown market cap, liquidity, beta or correlation blocks a trade. It is never treated as average, and an unmeasured beta counts as 1.5.
  • Append-only records. Strategy identity and version (league.py contains no UPDATE), decisions, the trial counter and accounting are never overwritten. Restatements sit beside originals with a version number.
  • Risk limits live apart from strategy config. config/risk.yaml is a separate file, so a strategy edit cannot widen a limit. LIVE is one line there, and changing it is the owner's step.
  • Idempotency. signal_id is a content hash and every broker order carries a fixed ref_id. After a restart, state is rebuilt from the orders ledger. Every order path (slots, Runners, options) uses the same engine.
  • The broker is authoritative. Every LIVE run reconciles the slot ledger against Robinhood's positions and halts on any difference.
  • A slot's P&L belongs to its current occupant. Slots get reassigned, and a trade from a previous occupant is never counted against the new one. A regression test pins this.
  • A filter that decides what may be bought never decides what may be sold. Exit prices come from the unfiltered series in the simulator, the null benchmark and the paper engine. This was learned three times (§18).

Integrity rules

  • Point in time. A filing becomes visible only from its first tradeable session after SEC acceptance. The SEC's "filed" date uses a 17:30 cutoff, so 48% of filings carry a date on which they were not tradeable; acceptance times are stored to the minute.
  • Freshness is measured at the source. The newest completed session comes from a liquid ETF at the data source, never from the table being updated. The freshness gate also checks derived tables and fails when one falls behind or is missing.
  • Absence is not zero. Missing debt, R&D or EPS stays missing, and any ratio built from it is missing too. Capex is subtracted in free cash flow.
  • Ticker hygiene. 212 large issuers had been mapped to a preferred or warrant series (JPM as JPM-PM); they were repaired narrowly. A dead company matched by ticker must agree with the company's own dates (426 of 676 "real dead" matches were impostors).
  • Adjusted prices. Prices are split- and dividend-adjusted, so they are never compared with raw option strikes or other unadjusted figures.

7Research factory

Strategy templates (Phase 13)

strategy_factory.py and its family files define strategies as economic hypotheses with small fixed parameter grids. Families include momentum (including the published 12-1 and 9-1 formations), value, quality, profitability, free cash flow to price, earnings surprise, insider buying, politician buying, short interest, calendar effects, mean reversion, short-term reversal, ETF rotation, bull/inverse pairs and multi-signal composites. Today: 88 families, 2,006 specs, 3,964 versioned strategies in the league.

The pipeline (factory_pipeline.py) moves a strategy through DISCOVERED → backtest → robustness (§28 collapse tests, predefined SPY regimes) → PAPER. Backtests admit a strategy to paper; only forward evidence can qualify it. discovery.py decides what to test next, with budgets and a failure log.

The Knowledge Factory (Stage O, complete)

Documented trading knowledge enters as hypotheses with provenance: 3,803 paper titles, 84 QuantConnect strategies, 40 curated entries and 153 published JKP factors, about 4,158 ideas in all. DeepSeek, used for development only and never in a scheduled job, translated rules into explicit fields, with a verification pass. Each source becomes a reproduction, then declared variants once the reproduction passes; lineage and a multiple-testing ledger are kept.

The audit's caveat stands: 91% of the 4,158 ideas are title-only and untestable, and the testable rest are about 175 lineages behind 1,281 strategies, so breadth is overstated. Two of the three medium-term slot holders are Knowledge Factory reproductions.

Published signals (Stage L)

JKP Global Factor Data (153 US factors, built on CRSP with dead companies included) and the OSAP signal documentation give a survivorship-free external reference. Post-publication long-leg excess survives at t > 3 for momentum, value, earnings yield and Piotroski. Liquidity and size are gone after publication.

The alpha bench (alpha_bench_v1) rebuilt 12 published signals long-only on our data: 0 validated, 8 replicated. Our long-short spreads correlate 0.86 (momentum) and 0.73 (value) with the published series.

The Lab: evolutionary search (frozen)

A genome represents entry and exit rule trees over the indicators plus tuned risk genes. The other components:

  • Simulator: fills at the next open, charges costs, lets gaps run through stops, and prices exits on the unfiltered series.
  • Fitness: risk-adjusted, net of costs, and measured as excess over a null surface across entry price × holding period, so neither market drift nor cheap stocks are rewarded.
  • Random-genome control: calibrates the gates against noise.
  • Six-stage promotion ladder: includes a sealed 2023-onward holdout, usable once per strategy.
  • Trial counter: 1,049,983 trials, 126,652 unique structures. It is append-only and never reset, so deflated Sharpe ratios stay honest.

The search found a series of artifacts (§18), produced 953 validation survivors that now paper-trade as the lab_search family (−1.03% per trade forward), and has been frozen by policy since 22 September. Only a human edit to search.mode can lift the freeze; the governor cannot.

The classifier

An XGBoost classifier over the 20 indicators has stable ranking skill: walk-forward AUC 0.629 over 31 rolling folds, 31 of 31 above 0.5. It does not have tradeable stock selection. Within a date its top decile does not beat its bottom decile, and under next-open fills it loses money gross. Its one surviving use is as a market-timing dial (§4, §14).

Experiments

Every experiment is registered in experiment_registry with its hypothesis, specification and pass rule hashed before results exist, and is completed once (§17). A caveat a reviewer should know: several were registered minutes before they ran, in the same session that wrote them. The spec is locked before the result is computed, but the gap is short, and crypto_trend_grid was registered after the fact and is labelled so.

8Dead companies (survivorship)

Free price data omits companies that died. Between 2006 and 2019 the price database holds 5 tickers that stopped trading, against 9,029 listings the Internet Archive shows existed; coverage of the knowable universe was 22.6% in 2008 and 83.7% in 2024. Since paid delisted data (~$270 a year) has not been bought (owner decision), the system reconstructs the missing companies:

  • Which companies existed: from SEC filings, the delisting registry and Internet Archive snapshots of the listing directory (Layer A, 1996–2024).
  • How each one ended: from its own 8-K items, grading 70% of exits as failure, buyout or SPAC.
  • A synthetic price path for each (generator v5c): failures copy real failure final years (80 real failures as the reference), buyouts copy real buyout years, and every row is tagged synthetic. Synthetic fundamentals come from the dead companies' own filings (4,093 companies), and a volume model was fitted on 228 real dead companies.
  • Three backtest modes: every ranked strategy is backtested in exclude (survivors only), as_is (with the synthetic dead) and zero (every unpriced death a total loss). The ranking scores on as_is and, under backtest_first, requires zero to be positive.

9Paper engine and accounting

  • Fills (since 10-21). The paper engine decides on the close and fills at the next open, the same convention as the simulator. Orders wait in paper_pending; sells fill first, gaps through stops are booked, and buys are sized at the open. An order with no open for 3 sessions is cancelled. Decision dates are kept, so the locked prospective test is not shifted twice.
  • Exits use the real close. A held stock that falls below the $5 purchase floor is still stopped on its real close; a stock with no bar is booked at its last real close.
  • Research mode. Funds hold up to 20 positions with $400 of capital (the 30 funds with early records kept $100).
  • Corporate actions. Open positions are re-anchored to the adjusted series (entry, stop and take-profit), so splits and dividends no longer book phantom gains or false stops.
  • Restated record. Trades filled at the signal-day close before 10-21 are restated at next-open fills in paper_trades_restated, beside the unchanged originals. 71,118 of 71,328 closed trades are restated; the remainder exit on the newest bar and have no next open yet.
  • Accounting v3 (since 10-22). Every fund is valued on a liquidation basis with gross, costs and net shown separately. Closed-trade P&L comes from the restated record; reconciliation stays on the engine's own ledger (2,793 of 2,804 funds reconciled on the first run).
  • Costs. $0 commission; spread by liquidity tier; 0.05% slippage per side; SEC and FINRA fees on sales; Robinhood's measured ~1.9% crypto round trip; options buy at the ask and sell at the bid.

Found by the rehearsal of the next-open engine (10-20). The old engine never stopped a held stock below the $5 purchase floor and booked it at its entry price after four holding periods. 35 closed trades were recorded at 0.00% when their true moves were −15% to −96% (mean −38%), and 701 open positions in 63 names (median −44%) were waiting to be booked the same way. Fixed; the sales were booked on 10-21/22. The paper record before 10-21 overstated returns by this amount.

10Ranking and slot selection

Ranking (ranking.py, daily [9i2])

2,609 strategies are assessed and 887 pass the gate (11-05). Gates:

  • A losing backtest is out.
  • The survivorship backtest's drawdown exposure must be within its limit.
  • The strategy must pass the unseen 2023-onward window, one look per strategy.
  • Under backtest_first (owner, 10-14) the worst-case (zero) backtest must also be positive, and Lab-search funds, which have no backtest, are ineligible.

The score is net return per 20 sessions held, so a year-long ETF trade and a week-long stock trade are on one scale. Holds under 5 sessions scale as 5.

In the default blend mode the score is (k × backtest + n × paper) / (k + n) with k = 10, so paper trades move it. In backtest_first mode, which is live, paper results are shown but never scored (§2).

Stop plans (stop_plans.py)

Every position needs a price stop before it can be bought. The stop comes from the strategy's genome, typically an ATR multiple. If several stops apply, the tightest wins, and risk exits override strategy signals. The genome's take-profit is carried as an exit, never as a stop, and a time limit applies. Stops are checked by the trader every 5 minutes, not held at the broker: Robinhood's dollar-amount orders are market orders only, so overnight gaps go through a stop.

11Live execution and risk

One LIVE trader run (every 5 minutes, 13:00–20:59 UTC weekdays)

  1. The trader writes a heartbeat row and checks the kill switches and the Robinhood sign-in.
  2. Split check: a clean broker-to-ledger quantity ratio, confirmed by a second source, restates the position (closing and reopening it at zero P&L) before any stop is checked.
  3. Monitor: each position is checked against its stop, take-profit, time limit and strategy exit. An exit on a name the strategy's entry rule still fires on is treated as a hold.
  4. Reconcile: the slot ledger is compared with Robinhood's positions, and any difference halts trading and alerts.
  5. Entry pass: empty slots are filled, retrying every 20 minutes while one is empty. An account-level cash refusal ends the pass.
  6. Drawdown tiers alert once a day per tier.

Every order goes through execution.py (duplicate check by content-addressed signal_id), then the risk engine, then robinhood_live.LiveBroker (review → place → confirm, with a fixed ref_id so a retry cannot double an order). An order whose outcome is unknown blocks new entries until it is resolved.

Quotes: Robinhood's last trade is used when fresh. If it is stale but the bid/ask is fresh and within 1%, the midpoint stands in (since 10-21).

21Questions for the reviewer

  1. Is "the machinery works, there is no edge" a fair reading? Or could something in the paper league make it look worse than it is? Two candidates: long-only exposure through the October–November decline, and the interim marks on open positions.
  2. Should forward evidence count in slot selection? Either as a release rule at the monthly review (a holder with 20 or more own trades, net negative, is released) or by returning to the blended score. Since 10-14 the ranking scores backtests only.
  3. Is the deciding bar right? The family test passes on 20 pooled entry dates plus 15 sessions with a max-T correction over about 28 effective tests. Does that have the power to see a realistic edge of ~0.5% a trade, or will a fail be uninformative?
  4. Single-name risk. Should basket slots (4 names, inverse-volatility) go live before the 20-session freeze ends? At $5 a leg, are they sensible at this account size?
  5. The Runners. Keep them live to their December verdict at $5 a trade, or move them to SHADOW now that the interim reading is negative?
  6. Drawdown tiers. Should the 15% review tier pause medium-term entries, rather than leaving that to the 20% tier?
  7. What next for research? Given the paper league's result, is further strategy research worth doing on this data without paid delisted data? Or is the better use of the system a forward-evidence engine, with live capital parked until something passes the gate?
  8. Options. Four option experiments have failed under the owner's long-only, one-contract rules, and the free data is thin. Is there a test worth running, or should options stay closed?
