Stockbot2000

An autonomous system that discovers, tests and ranks trading strategies, and puts the best-backtested ones in charge of three ~$20 slots in a real Robinhood account, with two more slots running same-day momentum Runners. This document gives an outside reviewer the architecture, methods, money, processes and open problems in enough depth to judge progress and advise on what comes next.

Design rules that bear load

  • Fail closed on unknowns. An unknown market cap, liquidity or correlation blocks a trade; it is never treated as average.
  • Append-only records. Strategy identity and version (league.py contains no UPDATE), decisions, the trial counter and accounting. Restatements sit beside originals with an accounting version.
  • Risk limits live apart from strategy config. config/risk.yaml is separate so that a strategy edit cannot widen a limit. LIVE is armed by one line there, and changing it is the owner's step.
  • Idempotency. signal_id is a content hash; broker orders carry a ref_id; after a restart the ledger is rebuilt from the orders table. The new options path (R7) reuses the identical pattern rather than inventing a second one.
  • Broker is authoritative. Every LIVE run reconciles the slot log against Robinhood positions and halts on any difference.
  • The Runners add no second order path. Every Runner order goes through the same ExecutionEngine, risk engine, kill switches and Robinhood adapter as the medium-term slots; exits bypass the order-count and loss throttles (so a stop can always fire), entries do not. A real-engine regression test (simulated broker, real database tables) covers a full entry, trailing-stop and exit cycle for each Runner.
  • A slot's P&L is scoped to its current occupant. Slots get reassigned to different strategies over time; a trade placed under the previous occupant must never be counted against the new one. Pinned by a regression test added this period (test_daily_snapshot.py).

Integrity rules (unchanged since last review)

  • Point in time. Fundamentals become visible only from the first session after SEC acceptance plus a 2-day lag. Market cap = shares from the filing × price on the filing date.
  • Freshness. The newest completed session is read from a liquid ETF at the source, never from the table being updated.
  • Absence is not zero. Missing debt, R&D or EPS stays missing, and any ratio built from it is missing too.
  • Backups. Forward and decision tables are dumped nightly to backups/forward.sql.gz, committed to git.

7Strategies and the ranking — what's new this period

Two stages finished building

Stage O, the Knowledge Strategy Factory, completed O1–O13 on 2026-10-05. Documented trading knowledge (academic papers, published factors, open-source strategy collections) now reproduces, varies within declared bounds, and is queued as seeds for governed machine mutation — inert until the evolutionary search is unfrozen, and still never importing the frozen search module directly (a regression test enforces the boundary). 153 JKP published factors are now seeded as KNOWN_FACTOR entries.

Stage R, options trading, completed R1–R7 on 2026-10-12. options_live.py mirrors a proven option strategy's newest paper trade for real: whole contracts, one leg at a time, the live ask, the same review-then-place-with-a-fixed-ref_id idempotency as the stock LIVE path. A regression bug was found and fixed while verifying it (the test's fake option price exceeded its own trade budget, so its "proven" scenario silently never fired). Built does not mean armed: allow_options is off, the account has no option level, and the best options paper record today is 3 closed trades against a 20-trade bar — so this is correctly inert, not merely unarmed.

The "seven positive families" of the 10-13 version, after correction

See §2. The seven families were positive on gross returns with trades treated as independent. On the corrected basis none qualifies (0 of 54). The audit's own explanation of why a t-statistic existed at all stands: nearly a thousand Lab validation survivors were enrolled in late September, with a start date of 09-24 that makes part of their record a replay.

Ranking and slot selection (changed 10-14)

Under evidence_mode: backtest_first (owner instruction 10-14: "the live slots must be the best performing ideas") a strategy is eligible only with a survivorship backtest that is positive even in the worst case (every unpriced death a total loss) and a pass on the unseen 2023-onward gate. It is scored on that backtest alone, per 20 sessions held. Paper results are displayed, never scored. Slots take the top five tradeable strategies, one per family, with the correlation limit (weekly returns, 0.7) and a rule that releases a holder the ranking predicts will lose. The previous formula, (k × backtest + n × paper) / (k + n) with k = 10, remains the default mode and can be restored by one config value.

Effect on 10-14: slots 1, 2, 4 and 5 were released and refilled (§2). Caveats a reviewer should weigh: backtests are one long, bull-biased window; the worst-case requirement removes 55% of strategies by construction; the new holders have almost no forward record; and the audit found backtest scores were a better predictor of later returns than the blended score had been (rank correlation +0.21 against +0.03), which is the argument for the change, not a demonstration that it pays.

14Questions for the reviewer

  1. The two Intraday Runners (new). They trade live under small caps with no demonstrated edge and the plan's gates waived. Is a capped live trial acceptable as the way to collect evidence, or should they run in SHADOW until the 60-session record the plan asked for exists? If they stay live, what is the right stopping rule (for example after N trades, or a cumulative loss limit, beyond the $2 and $1 daily caps)?
  2. Twin-ETF rule. Direction comes from the bull ETF's 15-minute momentum and the position is held up to 60 minutes with a 0.35% × leverage trailing stop. Index ETFs move far less intraday than stocks, and the project's earlier work found daily bull/bear switching loses to buy-and-hold. Is there any reason to expect 15-to-60-minute index continuation to survive costs, and what would you test first?
  3. Backtest-first selection (changed 10-14). Slots are now chosen on backtest alone, with a worst-case survivorship requirement and the unseen-2023 gate; paper results are shown, not scored. The audit found backtest rank correlated better with later returns than the blended score did (+0.21 against +0.03). Is that enough to justify ignoring forward records for selection, and should a forward record be a floor or a tiebreak?
  4. The corrected forward test finds nothing (0 of 54). The pre-registered prospective test has 2 of 20 dates. Should capital allocation wait for it, or is bounded exposure to the current top-ranked backtests defensible meanwhile?
  5. Reserving slots realised two losses (INTC, DELL). Should a reservation or reassignment wait for the holder's own exit, or is releasing at the next open the right default?
  6. Is "20 closed paper trades, net positive" the right bar for R7 (real-money options)? It is built and gated on exactly that bar; today's best is 3.
  7. Drawdown tolerance. The tiers (notice 10%, entries pause 20%, stop 35%) are set; the account is 9.6% below its peak. What loss would you want to see trigger a review of the whole approach, not just a pause?
  8. Synthetic dead companies (standing question). Is a tuned synthetic reconstruction acceptable for choosing strategies, or should the ranking gate on the worst case until real delisted prices are bought?
  9. Horizon comparability (standing question). How should strategies with 5-day, 60-day and option holds be put on one scale?
