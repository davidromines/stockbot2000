Three flaws the search found before we did

Both produced results that looked like discoveries. This is exactly the failure mode the project was warned about — a large search over noisy data will find the weaknesses in its own scoreboard first.

Neither was a coding slip that broke something visibly. Both silently made the numbers better, which is the dangerous kind.
FlawWhat it producedStatus
Variable shadowing
excess reused for node count		 Every excess P&L reported as 0; gates compared node counts against a dollar threshold Fixed
Horizon-mismatched benchmark
null fixed at 5 days		 Strategies earned "excess" by holding longer. Benchmark understated 32× at a 40-day hold. 107 of 200 passed validation on it Fixed
Price-blind benchmark
one null for the whole market		 The search's best answer was sma_200 < 8 — buy stocks under eight dollars. A price filter, not a strategy. 58 of 66 "survivors" were the same rule with a different constant. Understated 4.6× for cheap names Fixed

All 66 validation survivors are void (2026-09-12), along with the 200 shortlist entries behind them. Re-scored against the null of the stocks they actually bought, 0 of the top 25 still clear the gate. The best of them made +$26,166 in the simulator and had an excess of −$585 — every dollar of it was the benchmark. The verdicts are recorded as void, not fail: the strategies were never judged on correct terms, and are eligible to be judged again.

The null: what doing nothing clever earns

Buying at random was profitable in every window, because the market rose. Scoring against zero therefore measured market exposure, not skill — which is why 57% of random strategies used to pass validation. Every score now measures excess over this.

Survivorship bias

Measured with free data and subtracted from every backtest. Not fixed — fixing it needs prices for dead companies, which cost money.

Live trading has none of this. You buy what is listed today and the future is genuinely unknown. Survivorship only ever distorted the numbers used to choose a strategy — which is why measuring it mattered, and why ranking survives it even though absolute figures do not.

15 of 20 made money. 0 of 20 cleared the bar. That is the useful calibration: the gate is demanding but not impossible — one seed clears the excess leg, another clears the Sharpe leg, none clears both. trend_following_adx is the clearest proof the null works: +$1,173 earned, −$1,191 excess, because it is barely more than being long.
